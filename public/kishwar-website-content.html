2. Heritage Narrative

Heading: Where It Begins | Voice: First person

I was born and raised in Melbourne to Bangladeshi parents. My father, Kamrul Chowdhury OAM, came from Bikrampur in East Bengal. My mother, Laila, from Burdwan in West Bengal. They built a life in Australia and became pillars of the Bangladeshi community in Victoria - and they built it around food.

Every meal in our home was a lesson in who we are. My mother's kitchen was where I learned that food carries memory, identity, and belonging. A pot of khichuri on a rainy day was not just comfort - it was a connection to a country I had never lived in but somehow always knew.

I studied commerce at Monash University, then graphic design at the University of the Arts London. I lived in Germany, then spent six years in Bangladesh building a business. When I returned to Melbourne in 2015, I came home to cooking.

I staged at Ishizuka under Michelin-starred chef Masahiko Yomoda. I collaborated with Adam D'Sylva at TONKA on a modern Australian-Bengali menu. And in 2021, I walked onto the set of MasterChef Australia.

3. The MasterChef Story

Heading: MasterChef Australia

In the 2021 Grand Finale of MasterChef Australia Season 13, I cooked Smoked Rice Water - a modernised panta bhat, the fermented rice dish eaten by millions across Bengal. Judge Melissa Leong called it "powerful with history and powerful with flavour."

I finished 3rd, but something bigger happened. That dish went viral across Bangladesh and India. People who had grown up eating panta bhat - a humble, everyday food - saw it presented as worthy of the world's attention. Mothers sent me messages saying their children finally wanted to eat Bengali food.

That moment taught me what food can do. It can make people proud of where they come from.

4. Family

Heading: Family

My husband Ehtesham was my high school sweetheart. We have two children, Mikayle and Seraphina, who are growing up between two cultures - just like I did.

Everything I cook has them in it. The recipes in my cookbook are the dishes I want them to know how to make when they are older. Food is how we pass things on.

5. What I Do

Heading: What I Do

Today I work as a chef, author, and speaker. My debut cookbook, Smoke Rice Water, publishes with Hardie Grant in June 2026 - 100 recipes from Bengal that show this cuisine as it really is: light, fragrant, layered, and deeply personal.

I speak at events about heritage, food, cultural identity, and the migrant experience. I have delivered a TEDx talk, "Recipe for a Beautiful Mind," and serve as a Cricket Australia Multicultural Ambassador alongside Usman Khawaja, Mel Jones, and Wasim Akram.

I am an ambassador for ASRC Feast for Freedom and have worked with World Vision Australia to support child sponsorship in Bangladesh.

Page 4: Cookbook - "Smoke Rice Water"

Large book cover image (left) + text (right). Subtitle: Recipes and Stories from a Bengali Home.

2. Synopsis

Heading: About the Book

Bengali food feeds over 200 million people across Bangladesh and the Indian state of West Bengal. It is one of the most complex, fragrant, and layered cuisines in the world - and one of the most underrepresented in English-language cookbooks.

Smoke Rice Water brings 100 recipes from the Bengali kitchen into your home. Street snacks from the lanes of Dhaka. Mughal court dishes slow-cooked with centuries of technique. Fish stews that define the riverlands of Bengal. Sweets that mark every celebration. And the everyday meals that Kishwar's mother and grandmother made in their Melbourne kitchen - the recipes that kept a family connected to a country 12,000 kilometres away.

This is not a book about exotic food. It is a book about home.

3. The Story Behind the Name

Heading: Why "Smoke Rice Water"?

In the 2021 Grand Finale of MasterChef Australia, Kishwar cooked a dish called Smoked Rice Water - a modernised version of panta bhat, the traditional Bengali fermented rice eaten by farmers and families across Bangladesh for centuries.

Panta bhat is the simplest of foods: leftover rice soaked in water overnight, served with fried fish, green chillies, and onion. It is not a dish that wins competitions. But in Kishwar's hands, it did something more important - it made millions of people proud of their everyday food.

The name of this cookbook honours that moment, that dish, and that idea: the most ordinary ingredients can carry extraordinary meaning.

5. Author Note

Heading: A Note from Kishwar | Voice: First person

I wrote this book for my children. I wanted them to have a record of the food I grew up eating - the dishes my mother made without measuring, the recipes my grandmother knew by feel.

But I also wrote it for every Bengali family that has ever been told their food is "too simple" or "too unfamiliar." Bengali cuisine is neither. It is one of the most sophisticated food traditions in the world. This book is my attempt to show that.

I hope you cook from it, share it, and make these recipes your own.

Long Bio (300 words)

Kishwar Chowdhury is a Bangladeshi-Australian chef, author, and speaker based in Melbourne, Victoria. Born and raised in Melbourne to Bangladeshi parents, Kishwar grew up in a household where food was the primary language of identity and belonging.

Her father, Kamrul Chowdhury OAM, migrated from Bikrampur in East Bengal. Her mother, Laila, from Burdwan in West Bengal. Together they became pillars of the Bangladeshi community in Victoria, and it was in Laila's kitchen that Kishwar first learned to cook - and first understood that food carries memory, pride, and connection to home.

Kishwar studied commerce at Monash University, then graphic design at the University of the Arts London. She lived in Germany before spending six years in Bangladesh, where she ran a printing and promotional products business. After returning to Melbourne in 2015, she turned to cooking professionally - staging at Ishizuka under Michelin-starred chef Masahiko Yomoda and collaborating with Adam D'Sylva at TONKA on a modern Australian-Bengali menu.

In 2021, Kishwar competed on MasterChef Australia Season 13 and finished 3rd. Her Grand Finale dish, Smoked Rice Water - a modernised version of panta bhat, the humble fermented rice eaten by millions across Bengal - went viral across Bangladesh and India. Judge Melissa Leong called it "powerful with history and powerful with flavour." The dish made international headlines and gave millions of Bengali people a moment of deep cultural pride.

Since MasterChef, Kishwar has delivered a TEDx talk ("Recipe for a Beautiful Mind"), served as a Cricket Australia Multicultural Ambassador alongside Usman Khawaja, and cooked at Crown Melbourne as chef in residence. She is an ambassador for ASRC Feast for Freedom and World Vision Australia.

She lives in Melbourne with her husband Ehtesham and their two children, Mikayle and Seraphina. Her debut cookbook, Smoke Rice Water: Recipes and Stories from a Bengali Home, publishes with Hardie Grant on 23 June 2026.
